# The 45 second trailer

Forget the two minute walkthrough. That explained the game. A trailer does
not explain anything, it makes someone want to try it, and it only needs
**one** thing to go right on camera.

You record **one exchange with Dad**. That is all. Everything else is text
cards and the artwork you already have.

---

## What you have to capture

Exactly one clean take of this, on the first door:

1. The driver finishes speaking.
2. You hold `1`, say one line out loud, release.
3. Dad's voice says it back.

That is maybe twelve seconds. If it goes wrong, you have lost twelve seconds,
not a two minute run. Do it until one is clean.

**Say this**, because short and flat lands better than clever:

> Yeah, just leave it on the step.

Nothing else. Do not try the shout, do not try the contradiction, do not try
to get through the door. One exchange, then stop recording.

---

## The cut

Music under the whole thing. Something with a pulse, low, building. Cut the
cards on the beat.

| # | On screen | Audio | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Black | One knock. Then two more. | 2s |
| 2 | Black, white text: **You are twelve.** | music starts, low | 1.5s |
| 3 | Black, white text: **You are home alone.** | | 1.5s |
| 4 | Your cover art, slow push in on the door | | 2s |
| 5 | Game screen. The driver's line lighting up word by word | **his real audio** | 4s |
| 6 | Hard cut to black. Text: **They need an adult.** | music drops out | 1.5s |
| 7 | Game screen, the key goes down, your voice, the transcript appearing live | **your real voice**, unprocessed | 4s |
| 8 | Same screen, no cut, Dad's line lighting up | **Dad's voice**, full volume | 4s |
| 9 | Black. Text: **So be one.** | music back, harder | 2s |

**Shot 7 into shot 8 is the entire trailer.** Do not cut between them. The
viewer has to watch one continuous shot where a child's voice goes in and an
adult's comes out, because the moment they understand that, they want to try
it. Everything before is setup and everything after is credits.

Then, fast, no narration, one card each, under a second and a half:

| # | On screen |
|---|---|
| 10 | **Shout, and he shouts.** |
| 11 | **Lie, and the next one remembers.** |
| 12 | **Four doors. One night. One story.** |

Hold the last card an extra beat. Then:

| # | On screen |
|---|---|
| 13 | Cover art, title, **coverstory-eta.vercel.app** · Built on AssemblyAI |

Total: about 45 seconds.

---

## Why this works and the other one did not

The old script asked you to perform eight game beats live, in order, without
a mistake, while narrating. That is a thing a finished studio does with
twenty takes. You have one evening and a microphone.

This asks you for twelve good seconds. The rest is typography over a still
image, which cannot go wrong and which you can redo at two in the morning
without burning a run on the live link.

It is also just a better trailer. Game trailers do not demonstrate. They show
you one thing you have never seen before and then stop talking. Yours is a
twelve year old's sentence coming out of a grown man's mouth.

---

## If even that will not record

Shoot shots 5, 7 and 8 **silently**, screen only, and do the voices
afterwards: play the game, capture the screen, then re-record the audio
separately and lay it under. You are not faking anything, the game really
does this, you are just not fighting the microphone and the screen recorder
at the same moment.

---

## Recording setup

Use **Win+Alt+R**, the Windows Game Bar. It is built in and it does not take
the microphone away from the page.

**Turn Screenity off.** It is a browser extension that captures the
microphone, which is why the game kept dying on you: close code 1005, no
close frame, connection torn out locally. The game now survives that twice
per door, but the cleanest take is the one where nothing is fighting for the
input.

Headphones on, so Dad's voice does not go back into the microphone.
