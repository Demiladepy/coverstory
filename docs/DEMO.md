# Recording the demo

## The short version

The whole game is one gesture. **Hold a number key. Talk. Let go. Listen.**

That is it. Everything below is decoration on those four steps.

| Key | Who answers |
|-----|-------------|
| `1` | Dad |
| `2` | Mom |
| `3` | Uncle |
| `7` `8` `9` | A line you wrote earlier, spoken for you |

**Hold the key down the entire time you are speaking.** Not a tap. Press,
keep it pressed, say your sentence, then release. Nothing is listening before
you press or after you let go.

**Wait for the visitor to finish before you hold.** While they are still
talking the keys do nothing.

**After you let go, wait.** About half a second, then the grown up's voice
says your words back. If three seconds pass with nothing, the game now tells
you so and gives you the floor back.

**Four doors, one night.** You never click between them. The next visitor
knocks by themselves.

The rest of this page is a shot list for the video. You do not need it to
play.

---

Written as an order of operations: what to click, what to say, what to watch
for, in sequence. Follow it top to bottom and you have the video.

Target length **two minutes fifteen**. Judges watch a lot of these.

---

## Part 0. Before you press record

Six things, and skipping any of them costs you a take.

1. **Rehearse on localhost, record on the live link.** The live link allows
   five nights an hour. Four rehearsals and a take locks you out mid shoot.
   Run `npm run dev` and rehearse at `localhost:3200/play`, where the limit
   is sixty.
2. **Plug in headphones.** Without them the grown up's voice goes back into
   the microphone and the game scores you on it.
3. **Record microphone and system audio on separate tracks.** The viewer has
   to hear your real voice and then the grown up's, because the gap between
   those two is the entire product. Separate tracks let you set your voice
   slightly under and the house at full.
4. **Close the door and turn the fan off.** Room noise raises your measured
   baseline, and the shout is scored against that baseline.
5. **Open the live link and let the cover screen load once** before you
   record, so the first paint is not in the take.
6. **Know your lines.** Nothing below is faked. Every sentence is chosen so
   the beat lands on the first try.

## Part 1. The cover, six seconds, silent

**Click nothing.** Start recording with the opening screen already up, and
hold on it.

Watch the bottom of the frame: the two shadows in the light under the door
shift their weight and settle. Let that happen once. It is the only moment
in the video with nothing to read, and it is doing the work of a sentence
you now do not have to say.

Do not narrate over this.

---

## Part 2. Prep, ten seconds

**Click `Get your story straight`.**

Three slots appear, on keys 7, 8 and 9, each with a Dad / Mom / Uncle choice,
and a countdown running in the corner.

Slot 7 already has the cursor in it. **Type one line, exactly this:**

> He's in the shower, can you come back?

**Then click `Mom` on that row.** It opens on Dad, and the video has already
used Dad twice by the time this line fires, so switching it is worth the one
click. Leave 8 and 9 empty.

Filling one of three is faster on camera than filling three, and it shows
without a word that they are optional.

**Click `Let them knock`.** Do not wait the countdown out.

---

## Part 3. The first answer, eighteen seconds, still silent

Three knocks, then the driver speaks, and his words light one at a time as
he says them.

> **Driver:** "Evening. Large pepperoni, two garlic breads. I just need an
> adult to sign for it."

**Hold the `1` key down. Speak. Release `1`.**

> **You, out loud:** "Uh, yeah, just leave it on the step, mate."

Then stop touching the keyboard and let it play:

> **Dad's voice, out of the house:** *"Uh, yeah, just leave it on the step,
> mate."*

**Say nothing over any of this.** Eighteen seconds, no explanation, and the
viewer has understood the whole game. The same words lighting up in two
different voices explain it better than any sentence you could write.

---

## Part 4. One line of context, six seconds

Only now, a title card or one line spoken over the top:

> You are twelve. You are home alone. Four people are going to knock tonight.

Nothing else. No feature list, no architecture.

---

## Part 5. The shout, twenty seconds

Still the first door. **This must be your second answer of the night, not
your first.** The shout is measured against your own normal speaking level,
and at the first hold of a night the game has never heard you, so it falls
back to a default and the notch sits in the wrong place. By the second
answer it knows your voice.

Look at the short bar beside **DAD IS ANSWERING**. There is a notch on it.

**Hold `1`. Say this, and push the last word past the notch:**

> "He's in the shower, come back LATER."

**Release `1`.**

Three things land at once and all three are on screen, so hold the shot: the
bar crosses into amber, the live transcript keeps your raised word, and Dad
comes back with that word heavier and brighter in his mouth.

This is the most screenshot-able moment in the game. Do not cut away early.

---

## Part 6. The meter moves and says why, fifteen seconds

Get through the first door. The second visitor knocks by themselves; you do
not click anything between doors.

Wait for the neighbour to ask for somebody by name.

> **Neighbour:** "Is your mother in?"

**Hold `3`, not `2`.** That is Uncle answering a question about Mom.

> **Uncle's voice:** "She's upstairs, I'll get her."

The light under the door widens, a low tone falls, and **Wrong voice +18**
appears along the foot of the door.

One sentence over it:

> The meter is scored in code, so it can always tell you what you did.

---

## Part 7. The one that wins it, thirty five seconds

The cross door contradiction. This is the beat no other submission has, and
it needs setting up a door early.

**Back at door one, when the driver asked who was signing, you should
already have said:**

> "Dad's out in the garage, he'll sign."

**Now, at door two, hold `1` and say:**

> "Dad's at work till nine."

**Release.** The neighbour catches it:

> **Neighbour:** "But you said he was in the garage?"

Hold on **Doesn't add up +26** and the light going red.

One sentence over it:

> The model is not being asked whether that sounded suspicious. It is being
> asked one question against a record of everything the house has said all
> night.

**Make the clash blatant.** "In the garage" against "at work" is a clean
contradiction. "Busy" against "around" is not, and will not be caught,
because the judge runs conservative on purpose: punishing a player for
something they did not say is far worse than missing something they did.

---

## Part 8. The prepared line, eight seconds

At the next question, **tap `7`** and do not speak at all.

> **Mom's voice:** "I'm on a call, just leave it by the step."

One sentence:

> Written before I knew the question, so it buys a moment, not an answer.

---

## Part 9. The tape, twelve seconds

Play the night out. When it ends the end screen comes up by itself, and the
replay is already on it: you do not open anything.

**Click `Play it back`.** Let it run a few seconds. Each door is marked by
name, the visitor's lines sit left and the house's right, and the words light
across both voices in time with the audio.

**Then click `Download the tape`** and let the save appear. If you would
rather end on the link than on a file dialog, click `Share` instead, which
copies it.

> Every line of the night, with the word timings the speech API returned,
> stitched into one file.

---

## Part 10. Close, five seconds

Over the end screen, one breath each:

> Streaming speech to text hears me. The LLM Gateway writes the visitor and
> judges whether I contradicted myself. Text to speech is the whole cast.

Then the link, on screen and held.

## When a take goes wrong

**The visitor says something dull.** Re roll it. The line is generated fresh
every time, and two or three attempts at the opening beat is normal.

**The contradiction is not caught.** Make it blunter and go again.

**You get caught early.** Keep it. A night that ends in being caught is a
better video than a flawless one, because it proves the game can be lost,
and that is the only thing that makes surviving it mean anything. The end
screen says how many doors you got through.

**The meter does not move the way you expected.** Do not argue with it on
camera. Cut, and set the beat up again deliberately.

**Nothing comes back after you release the key.** Check the headphones are
actually selected as output. The commonest cause is the browser holding on
to the old device after you plugged them in mid session: reload the page and
allow the microphone again.
